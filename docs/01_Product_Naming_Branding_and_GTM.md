# PART 1 — PRODUCT NAMING

## 1.1 Naming brief

The product must communicate three things, in order of commercial importance:

1. **Proof / evidence** — DPDPA compliance is a burden-of-proof problem. When the Data Protection Board asks, the fiduciary must _demonstrate_ compliance, not assert it. This is also Axiom's differentiated back-half of the value loop (Discover → Assess → Report → **Remediate → Prove**).
2. **Agentic autonomy with human control** — agents do the work, the human approves. The name should not sound like a passive dashboard.
3. **India-first credibility** — the buyer is an Indian mid-market compliance head, CFO or COO, not a Silicon Valley CTO.

Naming constraints for a bootstrapped company: must be trademark-clearable in Class 42 (SaaS) and Class 45 (legal/compliance services), must have an available domain or clean subdomain under `axiomminds.ai`, and must not collide with Government of India programme names (a real risk with Sanskrit/Hindi compliance vocabulary).

## 1.3 RECOMMENDATION

> ### ✅ **Recommended product name: `Axiom Proof`**
>
> **Full brand lockup:** _Axiom Proof — by Axiom Minds_
> **Primary URL:** `https://axiomminds.ai/proof` (Phase 0) → `https://proof.axiomminds.ai` (Phase 2+)
> **Tagline:** _"Agents do the work. You approve. The proof is automatic."_

**Why this wins:**

1. **It names the differentiator, not the category.** Forty competitors are called some variant of "consent" or "comply". None are called _Proof_. In a market where the buyer's real fear is _"what do I show the Board when they ask?"_, owning "proof" is owning the anxiety.
2. **It compounds the company brand instead of competing with it.** A bootstrapped solo founder cannot afford to build two independent brands. Every unit of attention on "Axiom Proof" also builds "Axiom Minds" — critical when the founder's personal credibility _is_ the early GTM engine.
3. **It survives the services-to-software transition.** In Phase 1 the founder sells a service; in Phase 4 it's a platform. "Axiom Proof" works as the name of an engagement, a report, and a product — _"we ran an Axiom Proof assessment"_, _"here's your Axiom Proof evidence pack"_, _"we're on Axiom Proof."_
4. **Low legal risk, fast to launch.** No clearance blocker, no government-name collision, domain path already owned via `axiomminds.ai`.

**Backup name (if trademark counsel objects):** `ProofPlane` — retains the proof anchor with stronger inherent distinctiveness.

**Action:** file a word-mark application for **AXIOM PROOF** in Class 42 and Class 45 in Phase 0. Budget ₹15–25k including attorney fees.

# PART 2 — BRAND GUIDELINES

## 2.1 Brand positioning

**Category:** Agentic DPDPA compliance automation for Indian mid-market enterprises.

**Positioning statement:**

> _For Indian mid-market companies facing DPDPA enforcement without a privacy team, Axiom Proof is an agentic compliance platform where AI agents discover your personal data, assess your gaps, propose the fixes, and — once you approve — execute them with full audit trail and rollback. Unlike consent widgets that only flag problems, or enterprise suites that cost more than the fine, Axiom Proof does the work and hands you the proof._

**Brand promise:** _Agents propose. Humans approve. Agents execute. Everything is logged._

## 2.2 Brand personality

| Attribute      | We are                                               | We are not                                         |
| -------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------- |
| Tone           | Calm, precise, quietly confident                     | Alarmist, fear-selling, hype-driven                |
| Register       | Practitioner-to-practitioner                         | Vendor-to-prospect                                 |
| Stance on AI   | Agents as accountable colleagues under human command | "Magic AI", "fully autonomous", "no humans needed" |
| Stance on risk | Honest about limits; explicit about approval gates   | Overclaiming automation or coverage                |

**Critical brand rule:** Never market autonomy without control. Every piece of agentic messaging must pair the capability with the approval gate. _"Agents execute — after you approve"_ is the only correct construction. The Indian mid-market compliance buyer is risk-averse; unbounded AI autonomy is a **de-selection trigger**, not a selling point.

## 2.3 Visual identity

**Colour system**

| Role          | Colour      | Hex       | Use                                      |
| ------------- | ----------- | --------- | ---------------------------------------- |
| Primary       | Deep Indigo | `#1E2A4A` | Headers, primary surfaces, logo          |
| Accent        | Signal Teal | `#0FB5A5` | CTAs, agent-active states, approvals     |
| Proof Gold    | Seal Gold   | `#C9A227` | Evidence seals, verified/attested badges |
| Alert         | Ember       | `#D9534F` | Gaps, risks, overdue actions             |
| Neutral dark  | Slate       | `#2F3542` | Body text                                |
| Neutral light | Mist        | `#F4F6F8` | Backgrounds                              |

Rationale: indigo reads as institutional trust (BFSI/legal register); teal signals active machine intelligence; gold is reserved _exclusively_ for sealed evidence and attestations — it must never be used decoratively, so that gold always means "this is proven."

**Typography**

- Headings: **Inter Tight** (or Söhne if budget allows) — geometric, modern, authoritative
- Body: **Inter** — high legibility for dense compliance content
- Monospace (logs, audit trails, evidence hashes): **JetBrains Mono**

**Logo concept**
Wordmark "AXIOM PROOF" in Inter Tight Medium, with a mark composed of a **seal/checkmark formed from a chain link** — visually encoding the hash-chained audit ledger. Chain = traceability; seal = proof. Monochrome-first so it works on invoices, PDFs, and report covers cheaply.

**Report and evidence-pack design**
Because a large fraction of the product's perceived value is delivered as _documents_ (Board reports, auditor packs, DPB submissions), the report template is a first-class brand asset — arguably more important than the website in Phase 1. Every report carries: cover page with client name + assessment date + evidence-seal hash, agent attribution footer (_"Assessed by Parikshan · Evidence sealed by Saakshi · Reviewed and approved by [human name]"_), and a QR link to the verifiable audit trail.

# PART 3 — GO-TO-MARKET PLAYBOOK

## 3.1 GTM principle for a bootstrapped, agent-built company

Zero paid acquisition. Every rupee of CAC must be substituted with founder credibility, agent-generated content leverage, or partner economics that only pay out on closed revenue.

The unlock: **the same agents that serve customers also generate the marketing.** Nazar (regulatory watch) produces regulatory-change briefings; Sanket (market signal) identifies who is hiring DPOs or issuing privacy tenders; Parikshan can run anonymised aggregate assessments that become original research. A solo founder publishing genuinely original, machine-generated market intelligence weekly is a content operation that would otherwise need a three-person team.

## 3.2 Three channels

### Channel 1 — Founder network (Months 0–5, primary)

The founder's existing senior-executive and governance network is the highest-trust, zero-CAC channel and should be exhausted first.

- Target: 30–40 direct conversations in the first 60 days.
- Offer: free agent-run gap-scan as a conversation opener, not a pitch.
- Conversion goal: 3–5 paid engagements by Month 5.

### Channel 2 — Partner referral (Months 3–12, scaling)

CA firms, CS practices, boutique law firms, DPO consultancies and MSPs already hold the mid-market relationships and are being asked DPDPA questions they cannot answer technically.

- **Partner economics:** 15–20% referral fee on first-year contract value, or white-label delivery at 60–65% of list price. Pays out only on realised revenue — zero fixed cost.
- **Partner enablement kit:** one-page explainer, co-branded gap-scan landing page, sample evidence pack, objection-handling sheet.
- Target: 5–8 active referral partners by Month 12.

### Channel 3 — Organic content and Signal Lab (Months 0–ongoing, compounding)

- **Weekly LinkedIn cadence:** one regulatory-change note (from Nazar), one practitioner insight, one anonymised finding from real assessments.
- **Quarterly original research:** _"The Axiom Proof DPDPA Readiness Index"_ — aggregate anonymised findings across all assessments run. This is defensible, un-copyable content that no competitor without a customer base can produce, and it becomes the single best inbound asset by Month 9.
- **Free gap-scan** as the standing call-to-action on every piece.

## 3.3 The free gap-scan funnel

| Stage               | Mechanism                                            | Conversion assumption   |
| ------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------- |
| Awareness           | LinkedIn content, partner referral, network          | —                       |
| Gap-scan run        | Self-serve questionnaire; Parikshan generates report | 100% of entrants        |
| Report delivered    | Prioritised gaps + penalty exposure estimate         | ~100%                   |
| Consultation booked | Founder call to walk through findings                | 15–25% of scans         |
| Paid engagement     | Assessment project or vDPO retainer                  | 20–30% of consultations |

Net free→paid: realistically **3–7%**. The scan is a _conversation starter and credibility demonstration_, not a revenue engine. Keep its operating cost near zero — no live data connectors in Phase 1.

## 3.6 Objection handling

| Objection                                 | Response                                                                                                                                                |
| ----------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| _"Enforcement keeps slipping — why now?"_ | Discovery and remediation take 6–9 months. Starting at enforcement means being non-compliant at enforcement.                                            |
| _"You're one person."_                    | You're not buying headcount, you're buying an agent workforce with one accountable human reviewing every output. Here's the audit trail that proves it. |
| _"Why not Privy/OneTrust?"_               | They're built for enterprises with privacy teams. You don't have a privacy team — you need the work done, not another dashboard to staff.               |
| _"Can I trust AI to touch my systems?"_   | Agents never execute without your written approval, a dry-run result, and a rollback plan. You see the diff before anything changes.                    |
| _"We'll just use a consent banner."_      | A banner is one control out of forty-three. It won't answer a Board inquiry or a 72-hour breach report.                                                 |
